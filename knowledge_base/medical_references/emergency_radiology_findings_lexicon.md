# Emergency Radiology Lexicon: High-Yield Critical Findings & Imaging Signs

## 1. Thoracic Emergency Radiology

### A. Pneumothorax
- **Upright Chest Radiograph**:
  - Visceral pleural line visible with absence of peripheral bronchovascular lung markings.
- **Supine / Trauma Radiograph ("Deep Sulcus Sign")**:
  - Deepening, hyperlucency, and abnormal sharpness of the ipsilateral costophrenic angle extending toward the hypochondrium.
- **Tension Pneumothorax (Critical Emergency)**:
  - Ipsilateral complete lung collapse.
  - Contralateral shift of the mediastinum, trachea, and heart.
  - Depression / inversion of the ipsilateral hemidiaphragm.
  - *Clinical Mandate*: Immediate needle thoracostomy (2nd intercostal space midclavicular or 5th intercostal space anterior axillary) prior to chest tube placement.

### B. Acute Pulmonary Embolism (CTPA Signs)
- **Direct Signs**:
  - Intraluminal filling defect: Central, eccentric, or occlusive clot outlined by contrast ("polo mint sign" on cross-section; "railway track sign" on longitudinal views).
- **Secondary / Infarction Signs**:
  - **Hampton Hump**: Wedge-shaped, pleural-based peripheral triangular consolidation with its apex pointing toward the hilum.
  - **Westermark Sign**: Regional oligemia (focal hypoperfusion and pruning of pulmonary vessels distal to the occluded pulmonary artery).
  - **Palla Sign**: Enlargement of the right descending pulmonary artery.
- **Right Ventricular (RV) Strain Signs (Predicts Hemodynamic Collapse)**:
  - RV / LV diameter ratio > 1.0 on axial CT (measured at maximal ventricular dimension).
  - Flattening or paradoxical bowing of the interventricular septum toward the left ventricle.
  - Contrast reflux into the inferior vena cava (IVC) and hepatic veins.

### C. Acute Aortic Syndromes
- **Aortic Dissection**:
  - Intimal flap separating true lumen from false lumen.
  - *True lumen*: Usually smaller, earlier contrast opacification, continuous with aortic root.
  - *False lumen*: Larger, delayed enhancement, "beak sign", cobweb sign.
- **Intramural Hematoma (IMH)**:
  - Crescentic or circumferential hyperdense thickening of the aortic wall on non-contrast CT (>5 mm thickness) without an identifiable intimal flap.
- **Penetrating Atherosclerotic Ulcer (PAU)**:
  - Focal contrast ulceration penetrating through the internal elastic lamina into the aortic media, typically associated with extensive atherosclerotic plaque.

## 2. Abdominal & Gastrointestinal Emergencies

### A. Pneumoperitoneum (Free Intraperitoneal Air / Bowel Perforation)
- **Erect Chest / Abdomen X-ray**:
  - Crescentic subdiaphragmatic free air (most sensitive under the right hemidiaphragm).
- **Supine Signs on Plain Film**:
  - **Rigler Sign (Double Wall Sign)**: Both the inner (luminal) and outer (serosal) surfaces of the bowel wall are clearly outlined by gas.
  - **Football Sign**: Large volume of free gas outlining the entire peritoneal cavity in an oval shape, accentuating the falciform ligament.
  - **Lucent Liver Sign**: Decreased hepatic radiodensity due to gas overlying the anterior surface of the liver.
- **CT Features**:
  - Extraluminal gas bubbles near the site of perforation (most common: gastroduodenal peptic ulcer, diverticulitis, appendicitis).
  - Extravasation of oral contrast, focal bowel wall discontinuity, and localized phlegmon/free fluid.

### B. Acute Small Bowel Obstruction (SBO)
- **Radiographic Criteria**:
  - Small bowel dilation > 3.0 cm.
  - Plicae circulares extending completely across the lumen.
  - Multiple air-fluid levels with differential heights in the same bowel loop on upright/decubitus views.
  - String-of-pearls / string-of-beads sign: Small trapped gas bubbles between valvulae conniventes in fluid-filled loops.
- **CT Criteria for Strangulation / Closed-Loop (Surgical Emergency)**:
  - **Closed-Loop Obstruction**: Two points of obstruction along a single bowel segment ("U-shaped" or "C-shaped" loop).
  - **Whirl Sign**: Twisting of mesenteric vessels and bowel loops around the point of torsion.
  - **Ischemic Signs**: Reduced bowel wall enhancement, wall thickening (>3 mm), mesenteric edema/haziness, mesenteric venous thrombosis, and intramural gas (**pneumatosis intestinalis**).

## 3. Neurotrauma & Acute Stroke CT Signs

### A. Acute Intracranial Hemorrhage (Non-Contrast Head CT)
- **Hyperattenuation (Acute Blood)**: 50–80 Hounsfield Units (HU).
- **Epidural Hematoma (EDH)**:
  - Biconvex (lentiform), hyperdense extra-axial collection.
  - Does NOT cross cranial sutures (limited by periosteal attachments); CAN cross dural venous sinuses and tentorium.
  - Typically arterial (middle meningeal artery rupture associated with temporal bone fracture).
- **Subdural Hematoma (SDH)**:
  - Crescentic (concave-inner, convex-outer) extra-axial collection extending along the cerebral convexity.
  - CAN cross cranial suture lines; does NOT cross dural attachments (falx cerebri or tentorium).
  - Venous origin (tearing of bridging cortical veins).
- **Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH)**:
  - Hyperdensity conforming to the basal cisterns, sylvian fissures, and cortical sulci.
  - Modified Fisher Scale grades vasospasm and delayed cerebral ischemia risk.

### B. Early Ischemic Stroke Signs (Non-Contrast CT within 6 hours)
- **Hyperdense Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA) Sign**: Increased density of the M1/M2 segment indicating acute thromboembolic occlusion.
- **Insular Ribbon Sign**: Loss of gray-white matter differentiation at the insular cortex.
- **Obscuration of the Lentiform Nucleus**: Loss of definition of the putamen and globus pallidus.
- **Sulcal Effacement**: Focal cortical swelling due to cytotoxic edema.
- **ASPECTS Score (Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score)**: Quantitative 10-point scoring of the MCA territory; score ≤ 7 indicates large-core infarct with increased hemorrhagic transformation risk.
