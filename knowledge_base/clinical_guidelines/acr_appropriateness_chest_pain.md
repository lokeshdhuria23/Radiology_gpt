# American College of Radiology (ACR) Appropriateness Criteria: Acute Chest Pain

## Clinical Overview
Acute chest pain is one of the most common acute presentations requiring rapid triage to rule out life-threatening causes (the "Big Four"):
1. Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS / Myocardial Infarction)
2. Acute Pulmonary Embolism (PE)
3. Acute Aortic Syndrome (Aortic Dissection, Intramural Hematoma)
4. Tension Pneumothorax or Esophageal Rupture (Boerhaave Syndrome)

---

## 1. Initial Radiographic Evaluation
- **Chest Radiograph (CXR, PA and Lateral or AP Portable)**:
  - **Rating**: Usually Appropriate (Score 9/9)
  - **Clinical Utility**: Initial screening for pneumothorax, pneumonia, pulmonary edema, widened mediastinum (>8 cm suggestive of aortic pathology), rib fractures, and pneumomediastinum.
  - **Limitation**: Normal CXR does NOT exclude pulmonary embolism or acute coronary syndrome.

---

## 2. Suspected Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS)
- **Low-to-Intermediate Risk Patients (TIMI 0-2, HEART score ≤ 3, negative serial troponins, non-diagnostic ECG)**:
  - **Coronary CT Angiography (CCTA)**:
    - **Rating**: Usually Appropriate (Score 8/9)
    - **Criteria**: Heart rate optimized (<65 bpm preferred), eGFR > 30 mL/min, able to hold breath 10-15 seconds.
    - **Negative Predictive Value**: >99% for excluding obstructive coronary artery disease.
- **High-Risk ACS / STEMI / NSTEMI**:
  - Urgent invasive coronary catheterization; CT angiography is NOT indicated and should not delay reperfusion.

---

## 3. Suspected Acute Pulmonary Embolism (PE)
- **Clinical Pre-test Probability (Wells Score or Geneva Score)**:
  - **Low Probability + PERC Negative**: No imaging or D-dimer required.
  - **Low/Intermediate Probability + Elevated D-dimer (>500 ng/mL or age-adjusted)**:
    - **CT Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA)**:
      - **Rating**: Usually Appropriate (Score 9/9)
      - **Protocol**: Contrast bolus timed to pulmonary arterial trunk (100-120 kVp, submillimeter slice thickness).
  - **High Probability (Wells > 6)**:
    - Proceed directly to CTPA without D-dimer testing.
- **Contraindications to Iodinated Contrast (Severe allergy or severe renal failure eGFR < 30)**:
  - **V/Q Lung Scan (Ventilation-Perfusion Scintigraphy)**:
    - **Rating**: Usually Appropriate (Score 8/9) if baseline CXR is normal.
  - **Lower Extremity Venous Duplex Ultrasound**:
    - **Rating**: Appropriate adjunct; finding proximal DVT treats PE without requiring thoracic CT.

---

## 4. Suspected Acute Aortic Syndrome (Dissection / Hematoma)
- **Clinical Presentation**: Sudden onset tearing or ripping chest pain radiating to the back, pulse deficits, blood pressure differential between arms (>20 mmHg).
- **Imaging of Choice**:
  - **CT Angiography Chest, Abdomen, and Pelvis with and without IV Contrast**:
    - **Rating**: Usually Appropriate (Score 9/9)
    - **Protocol**: Non-contrast phase (identifies hyperdense intramural hematoma), followed by arterial phase extending from thoracic inlet to femoral bifurcations.
    - **Stanford Classification**:
      - **Type A**: Involves ascending aorta (surgical emergency).
      - **Type B**: Involves descending aorta distal to left subclavian artery (medical management unless complicated by malperfusion or rupture).
  - **Transesophageal Echocardiography (TEE)**:
    - **Rating**: Usually Appropriate (Score 8/9) for hemodynamically unstable patients unable to travel to CT scanner.

---

## 5. "Triple Rule-Out" CT Protocol
- **Definition**: ECG-gated volumetric single-acquisition CT angiography covering coronaries, pulmonary arteries, and entire thoracic aorta.
- **Indications**: Indeterminate acute chest pain where clinical evaluation cannot clearly distinguish between ACS, PE, and Aortic Dissection.
- **Trade-off**: Higher contrast volume (90-120 mL) and higher radiation dose compared to standard targeted CTPA.
