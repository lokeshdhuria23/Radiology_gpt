# Fleischner Society 2017 Guidelines for Incidental Pulmonary Nodules

## Scope & Applicability
These guidelines apply to incidental solid and subsolid pulmonary nodules detected on CT in patients **aged 35 years or older**.
- **Exclusions**: Patients with known primary cancer, immunocompromised patients, or patients undergoing lung cancer screening (who should be evaluated under Lung-RADS).

---

## 1. Solid Nodules: Management by Size and Clinical Risk

Clinical Risk Factors for Malignancy:
- High risk: Older age, heavy smoking history, family history of lung cancer, upper lobe location, spiculation, larger size, female sex.
- Low risk: Young patient, minimal or no smoking, smooth margins, small size.

### A. Single Solid Nodule
| Nodule Size | Low Risk Patient | High Risk Patient |
|---|---|---|
| **< 6 mm** (< 100 mm³) | No routine follow-up needed | Optional CT at 12 months in select cases |
| **6 to 8 mm** (100–250 mm³) | CT at 6–12 months; consider repeat at 18–24 months if suspicious | CT at 6–12 months, then repeat CT at 18–24 months |
| **> 8 mm** (> 250 mm³) | Consider CT at 3 months, PET/CT, or tissue biopsy | CT at 3 months, PET/CT, or biopsy based on morphology & clinical suspicion |

### B. Multiple Solid Nodules
| Nodule Size | Low Risk Patient | High Risk Patient |
|---|---|---|
| **< 6 mm** | No routine follow-up | Optional CT at 12 months |
| **≥ 6 mm** | CT at 3–6 months; subsequent CT at 18–24 months based on risk | CT at 3–6 months, then repeat at 18–24 months |

*Note: For multiple nodules, follow-up intervals are dictated by the most suspicious/largest nodule.*

---

## 2. Subsolid Nodules (Ground-Glass and Part-Solid)

*Note: Subsolid nodules carry a higher baseline risk of malignancy (adenocarcinoma spectrum), but grow significantly more slowly than solid nodules.*

### A. Solitary Pure Ground-Glass Nodule (GGN)
| Nodule Size | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **< 6 mm** | No routine follow-up needed |
| **≥ 6 mm** | CT at 6–12 months to confirm persistence, then repeat CT every 2 years until 5 years |

### B. Solitary Part-Solid Nodule
| Nodule Feature | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **< 6 mm total size** | No routine follow-up needed |
| **≥ 6 mm total with solid component < 6 mm** | CT at 3–6 months to confirm persistence; if persistent and solid component remains < 6 mm, annual CT for 5 years |
| **Solid component ≥ 6 mm** | High suspicion for invasive adenocarcinoma. CT at 3–6 months or PET/CT / biopsy / surgical resection |

### C. Multiple Subsolid Nodules
| Feature | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **< 6 mm** | CT at 3–6 months; if stable, consider CT at 2 and 4 years |
| **≥ 6 mm** | CT at 3–6 months. Subsequent management based on the most suspicious nodule(s) |

---

## 3. High-Risk Morphological Features
When present, these features escalate the recommendation to closer follow-up or biopsy regardless of size:
- **Corona radiata / Spiculation**: Spiculated margins carry a likelihood ratio of malignancy > 2.5.
- **Upper lobe predominance**: Upper lobes have a 2-3x higher probability of primary lung carcinoma.
- **Growth rate**: Volume doubling time (VDT) < 400 days strongly indicates malignancy. VDT < 20-30 days suggests infection/inflammation. VDT > 500 days suggests benign lesion or indolent adenocarcinoma in situ.
- **Cavitation**: Thick-walled cavities (>15 mm wall thickness) are malignant in 84% of cases.
