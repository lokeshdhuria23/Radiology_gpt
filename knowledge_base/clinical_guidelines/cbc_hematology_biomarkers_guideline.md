# Complete Blood Count (CBC) and Inflammatory Biomarkers Clinical Reference

## 1. Complete Blood Count (CBC) Reference Intervals & Clinical Significance

### White Blood Cell (WBC) & Differential
- **Total WBC Count**: 4.0 – 11.0 × 10³/µL (4,000 – 11,000 /µL)
  - *Leukocytosis (> 11.0 × 10³/µL)*: Acute bacterial infection, tissue necrosis, severe systemic inflammation, physical/metabolic stress, corticosteroid therapy, or myeloproliferative disorders.
  - *Marked Leukocytosis (> 15.0 × 10³/µL)*: High clinical suspicion for invasive bacterial process or occult sepsis cascade.
  - *Leukopenia (< 4.0 × 10³/µL)*: Viral suppression (influenza, EBV, CMV, HIV), bone marrow infiltration, autoimmune neutropenia, or severe sepsis with marrow exhaustion.
- **Neutrophils (Segmented)**: 40% – 74% (Absolute: 2.0 – 8.0 × 10³/µL)
  - Primary defense against acute bacterial infections. Left shift (bandemia > 10%) signifies active granulopoiesis in response to severe infection.
- **Lymphocytes**: 20% – 48% (Absolute: 1.0 – 4.8 × 10³/µL)
  - Mediators of viral immunity and adaptive responses. Lymphopenia (< 1.0 × 10³/µL) correlates with acute viral stress, sepsis severity, or immunodeficiency.
- **Monocytes**: 3% – 11% (Absolute: 0.16 – 1.0 × 10³/µL)
  - Phagocytic cells involved in chronic infection, recovery phases, and granulomatous disease.
- **Eosinophils**: 0% – 8% (Absolute: 0.02 – 0.8 × 10³/µL)
  - Elevated in allergic hypersensitivity, parasitic helminth infections, drug reactions (DRESS syndrome), and eosinophilic asthma.
- **Basophils**: 0% – 1.5% (Absolute: 0.0 – 0.2 × 10³/µL)
  - Involved in immediate hypersensitivity and histamine release.

### Red Blood Cell Indices & Oxygen Transport
- **Total Red Blood Cell (RBC)**: 3.8 – 5.2 × 10⁶/µL (females), 4.3 – 5.9 × 10⁶/µL (males)
- **Hemoglobin (HGB)**: 12.0 – 16.0 g/dL (females), 13.5 – 17.5 g/dL (males)
  - *Mild Anemia*: 10.0 – 11.9 g/dL
  - *Moderate Anemia*: 8.0 – 9.9 g/dL (presents with fatigue, exertional dyspnea, pallor, mild tachycardia)
  - *Severe Anemia*: < 8.0 g/dL (critical transfusion evaluation threshold, hemodynamic instability)
- **Hematocrit (HCT)**: 36% – 48% (females), 40% – 52% (males)
- **Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV)**: 80 – 99 fL
  - *Microcytic (< 80 fL)*: Iron deficiency anemia, thalassemia trait, anemia of chronic disease, lead toxicity.
  - *Normocytic (80 – 99 fL)*: Acute blood loss, hemolysis, anemia of chronic disease/renal failure, early mixed deficiency.
  - *Macrocytic (> 99 fL)*: Vitamin B12 deficiency, folate deficiency, alcohol excess, liver disease, myelodysplasia, hydroxyurea therapy.
- **Red Cell Distribution Width (RDW)**: 11.5% – 15.0%
  - Elevated RDW (> 15.0%) reflects anisocytosis (variable cell size), common in early iron deficiency prior to overt MCV decline.

### Platelet Indices & Hemostasis
- **Platelet Count (PLT)**: 150 – 450 × 10³/µL
  - *Mild Thrombocytopenia*: 100 – 149 × 10³/µL (generally asymptomatic)
  - *Moderate Thrombocytopenia*: 50 – 99 × 10³/µL (bleeding precautions indicated; avoid NSAIDs, antiplatelets, IM injections)
  - *Severe Thrombocytopenia*: < 50 × 10³/µL (spontaneous petechiae, ecchymosis, mucosal hemorrhage; urgent hematology consult)
  - *Critical Spontaneous Bleeding Risk*: < 20 × 10³/µL

## 2. Inflammatory Biomarkers & Sepsis Stratification
- **C-Reactive Protein (CRP)**: Normal < 5.0 mg/L (or < 0.5 mg/dL)
  - Hepatic acute-phase reactant synthesized in response to interleukin-6.
  - > 50 mg/L indicates acute systemic bacterial infection, sepsis, severe trauma, or active systemic vasculitis.
- **Procalcitonin (PCT)**: Normal < 0.1 ng/mL
  - Highly specific for invasive bacterial infection with systemic involvement.
  - 0.5 – 2.0 ng/mL: Sepsis is possible; consider empiric antibiotics.
  - > 2.0 ng/mL: High risk of severe sepsis / septic shock; requires immediate broad-spectrum coverage and fluid resuscitation.

---

## 3. Patient-Friendly Translation Guidelines
When communicating laboratory abnormalities to patients:
1. Demystify technical abbreviations (e.g. WBC = infection-fighting cells; Hgb = oxygen carrier protein; PLT = clotting cell fragments).
2. Clearly explain whether an abnormal number indicates an active battle (infection), tiredness (anemia), or clotting need (platelets).
3. Pair every abnormal finding with reassuring, actionable nutrition, vitamin, and hydration steps.
4. Establish clear red flags for immediate medical re-evaluation.
