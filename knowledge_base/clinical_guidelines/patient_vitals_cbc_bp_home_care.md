# Patient Health Reference Guide: Blood Count (CBC), Blood Pressure (BP), Glucose, & Vitals

## 1. Complete Blood Count (CBC) with Differential — Patient Quick Guide

The Complete Blood Count measures the cells circulating in your bloodstream. Here are standard adult reference ranges, what low or high numbers signify, and practical home care guidance:

### White Blood Cells (WBC) — Immune Defense
- **Normal Range**: 4.0 – 11.0 × 10³/µL (or 4,000 – 11,000 cells/µL)
- **High WBC (> 11.0 × 10³/µL, Leukocytosis)**:
  - *What it means*: The body's immune system is actively responding to a bacterial infection, viral challenge, severe allergic reaction, tissue injury, or physical stress.
  - *Home Care*: Rest, drink at least 2 to 3 liters of fluids (water, electrolyte solutions, warm broths), avoid strenuous exertion, monitor temperature twice daily.
  - *Doctor Red Flag*: Fever above 102.5°F (39.2°C), severe chills, stiff neck, productive cough with dark phlegm, or fast heart rate.
- **Low WBC (< 4.0 × 10³/µL, Leukopenia)**:
  - *What it means*: Reduced infection-fighting capability; can occur from viral infections, autoimmune conditions, medications, or bone marrow suppression.
  - *Precaution*: Practice rigorous hand hygiene, avoid crowded spaces, cook all meats thoroughly, wash raw produce.

### Neutrophils & Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC)
- **Normal Percentage**: 40% – 75%
- **Normal Absolute Count (ANC)**: 1.8 – 8.0 × 10³/µL
- **High Neutrophils (Neutrophilia)**: Classic marker of acute bacterial infection or systemic inflammation.
- **Low Neutrophils (Neutropenia < 1.5 × 10³/µL)**: High infection risk; fever with low neutrophils requires immediate emergency care.

### Lymphocytes
- **Normal Range**: 20% – 48% (Absolute: 1.0 – 4.8 × 10³/µL)
- **High**: Frequently seen during viral recovery (mononucleosis, influenza, COVID-19).
- **Low**: Often seen in acute bacterial sepsis, steroid use, or intense stress.

### Red Blood Cells (RBC) & Hemoglobin (Hgb) — Oxygen Transport
- **Normal Hemoglobin**:
  - Men: 13.8 – 17.2 g/dL
  - Women: 12.0 – 15.5 g/dL
- **Low Hemoglobin (< 12.0 g/dL, Anemia)**:
  - *What it means*: Fewer red blood cells carrying oxygen to your organs. Common symptoms include fatigue, pale skin, cold hands/feet, weakness, and mild dizziness on standing.
  - *Nutrition & Self-Care*:
    - Eat iron-rich foods: Lentils, spinach, beans, fortified cereals, lean poultry, tofu.
    - Pair iron with Vitamin C (oranges, tomatoes, bell peppers) to boost absorption by up to 300%.
    - Avoid drinking tea or coffee within 1 hour of meals, as tannins inhibit iron absorption.
  - *Doctor Red Flag*: Chest pain, shortness of breath while resting, black or bloody stools, or feeling faint.

### Platelets (PLT) — Blood Clotting
- **Normal Range**: 150 – 450 × 10³/µL
- **Low Platelets (< 150 × 10³/µL, Thrombocytopenia)**:
  - *What it means*: Slower blood clotting; increased bruising or minor bleeding.
  - *Precaution*: Use a soft-bristle toothbrush, avoid hard contact sports, blow your nose gently, and avoid aspirin or non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs like ibuprofen) unless specifically instructed by your physician.
  - *Doctor Red Flag*: Spontaneous nosebleeds lasting >15 minutes, blood in urine or stool, or tiny pinpoint red/purple spots on legs (petechiae).
- **High Platelets (> 450 × 10³/µL, Thrombocytosis)**: Reactive response to iron deficiency, inflammation, or infection.

## 2. Blood Pressure (BP) Classification & Precautions

Blood pressure is recorded as Systolic (heart pumping) over Diastolic (heart resting between beats) in mmHg.

| Category | Systolic (mmHg) | | Diastolic (mmHg) | Action & Home Precautions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | Less than 120 | and | Less than 80 | Maintain healthy lifestyle: balanced diet, regular physical activity, moderate salt intake. |
| **Elevated** | 120 – 129 | and | Less than 80 | Adopt DASH diet principles, reduce daily sodium to < 2,300 mg, increase dietary potassium (bananas, potatoes). |
| **Stage 1 High BP** | 130 – 139 | or | 80 – 89 | Lifestyle modification; consult doctor regarding cardiovascular risk assessment and possible medication. |
| **Stage 2 High BP** | 140 or higher | or | 90 or higher | Consult doctor promptly. Typically requires 1 or 2 blood pressure medications; avoid high-sodium processed foods. |
| **Hypertensive Crisis** | Higher than 180 | and/or | Higher than 120 | **Emergency Medical Evaluation Required immediately** if accompanied by chest pain, shortness of breath, blurry vision, or numbness. |

### How to Measure Blood Pressure Accurately at Home:
1. Rest quietly in a chair for 5 minutes before taking the measurement.
2. Keep your feet flat on the floor; do not cross legs.
3. Support your arm on a table so the cuff sits level with your heart.
4. Avoid caffeine, exercise, and smoking for 30 minutes beforehand.
5. Take two readings 1 minute apart and record the average.

## 3. Blood Glucose & Metabolic Ranges

- **Fasting Blood Sugar (FPG)**:
  - Normal: 70 – 99 mg/dL
  - Prediabetes: 100 – 125 mg/dL
  - Diabetes: 126 mg/dL or higher on two separate tests
- **Post-Meal Blood Sugar (2 hours after eating)**:
  - Normal: Under 140 mg/dL
  - Prediabetes: 140 – 199 mg/dL
  - Diabetes: 200 mg/dL or higher
- **Hemoglobin A1c (3-Month Average Glucose)**:
  - Normal: Below 5.7%
  - Prediabetes: 5.7% – 6.4%
  - Diabetes: 6.5% or higher
- **Hypoglycemia (Low Blood Sugar < 70 mg/dL)**:
  - *Symptoms*: Shakiness, cold sweating, fast heartbeat, dizziness, confusion.
  - *Rule of 15*: Consume 15 grams of fast-acting sugar (4 oz fruit juice or 3-4 glucose tablets), wait 15 minutes, and recheck blood sugar.

## 4. Body Temperature, Fever & Cold Guidelines

- **Normal Temperature**: 97.0°F – 99.0°F (36.1°C – 37.2°C)
- **Low-Grade Fever**: 99.1°F – 100.4°F (37.3°C – 38.0°C)
- **Moderate Fever**: 100.5°F – 102.2°F (38.1°C – 39.0°C)
- **High Fever**: 102.3°F – 104.0°F (39.1°C – 40.0°C)
- **Emergency Fever**: > 104.0°F (> 40.0°C)

### Safe Home Care & Over-the-Counter Guidance:
- **Hydration**: Essential. Drink water, oral rehydration salts (ORS), and warm teas.
- **Acetaminophen (Paracetamol/Tylenol)**: 500 mg every 4–6 hours as needed (do not exceed 3,000 mg in 24 hours).
- **Ibuprofen (Advil/Motrin)**: 200–400 mg every 6–8 hours with meals (avoid if you have stomach ulcers, kidney disease, or an NSAID allergy).
- **Vitamins**: Vitamin C (500–1000 mg), Zinc (15–30 mg), and Vitamin D3 support immune defense during viral recovery.
- **Urgent Care Thresholds**: Fever lasting > 3 days, difficulty breathing, stiff neck, or extreme lethargy.
