# Clinical Drug-Drug Interactions: High-Risk Combinations & Prescribing Alerts

## 1. Critical Drug Interactions (Life-Threatening / Hospitalization Risk)

### A. Anticoagulants / Antiplatelets + Nonsteroidal Anti-inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs)
- **Interacting Classes**:
  - Anticoagulants: Warfarin, Apixaban, Rivaroxaban, Dabigatran, Enoxaparin.
  - Antiplatelets: Aspirin, Clopidogrel, Ticagrelor.
  - NSAIDs: Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Ketorolac, Meloxicam, Celecoxib, Diclofenac.
- **Mechanism**:
  - NSAIDs inhibit COX-1-mediated gastroprotective prostaglandins (causing gastric mucosal erosion) and reversibly inhibit platelet aggregation.
  - Combined with systemic anticoagulation, this yields a **3- to 6-fold increase in major gastrointestinal bleeding** and intracranial hemorrhage.
- **Clinical Rule**:
  - Avoid concurrent prescription. For acute musculoskeletal pain in anticoagulated patients, use Acetaminophen (max 2–3 g/day) or topical agents.
  - If NSAID is mandatory (e.g., acute pericarditis), co-prescribe a Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI: Pantoprazole 40 mg daily) and minimize treatment duration.

---

### B. Dual Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS) Blockade
- **Interacting Classes**: ACE Inhibitors (Lisinopril, Ramipril) + Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (Losartan, Valsartan) OR Direct Renin Inhibitors (Aliskiren).
- **Mechanism**: Excessive inhibition of efferent arteriolar tone in renal glomeruli leads to acute reduction in intraglomerular filtration pressure.
- **Adverse Outcome**: Acute renal failure, refractory hyperkalemia, and cardiovascular syncope without added mortality benefit.
- **Clinical Rule**: Strict contraindication. Never combine ACEi with ARB.

---

### C. RAAS Inhibitors + Potassium-Sparing Diuretics / Potassium Supplements
- **Combinations**:
  - Lisinopril / Losartan / Sacubitril-Valsartan + Spironolactone / Eplerenone / Amiloride / Triamterene / Oral Potassium Chloride (K-Dur).
- **Adverse Outcome**: **Severe Hyperkalemia (K+ > 6.0 mEq/L)** leading to cardiac conduction blocks, sine-wave ECG, ventricular fibrillation, and cardiac arrest.
- **Clinical Rule**:
  - If combined for heart failure GDMT (e.g., ARNI + Spironolactone), check serum potassium and creatinine at baseline, 1 week, 4 weeks, and every 3–6 months.
  - If K+ > 5.5 mEq/L: Halve the spironolactone dose. If K+ > 6.0 mEq/L: Stop spironolactone immediately and administer emergent potassium-lowering therapy.

---

### D. Metformin + Iodinated Radiologic Contrast Media
- **Adverse Outcome**: Contrast-Induced Nephropathy (CIN) leads to acute metformin accumulation and **Metformin-Associated Lactic Acidosis (MALA)**, with an estimated mortality rate of 30–50%.
- **Clinical Rule**:
  - Withhold metformin prior to or at time of iodinated contrast in patients with eGFR < 60 mL/min, hepatic impairment, alcoholism, or acute sepsis.
  - Verify stable renal function at 48 hours post-scan prior to re-initiating.

---

### E. QT-Prolonging Combinations (Torsades de Pointes Risk)
- **Combinations**:
  - Antiarrhythmics: Amiodarone, Sotalol.
  - Antibiotics / Antifungals: Azithromycin, Levofloxacin, Ciprofloxacin, Fluconazole.
  - Psychotropics: Haloperidol, Citalopram, Escitalopram, Quetiapine.
  - Antiemetics: Ondansetron (Zofran) IV high dose.
- **Adverse Outcome**: Additive prolongation of corrected QT interval (QTc > 500 ms) triggering polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (Torsades de Pointes).
- **Clinical Rule**:
  - Baseline ECG QTc calculation. Avoid prescribing >1 non-essential QTc-prolonging agent. Correct underlying hypokalemia (K+ < 4.0) and hypomagnesemia (Mg2+ < 2.0 mg/dL).

---

## 2. Common Medication Duplication Alerts
- **Dual NSAID Use**: e.g., Patient taking over-the-counter Ibuprofen while prescribed Meloxicam. Increases GI ulceration risk without incremental analgesic benefit.
- **Dual Statin Therapy**: e.g., Atorvastatin + Simvastatin. Increases risk of rhabdomyolysis and transaminitis.
- **Dual Benzodiazepine or Sedative Use**: e.g., Alprazolam + Lorazepam or Zolpidem + Temazepam. Substantially elevates risk of CNS/respiratory depression, falls, and oversedation.
