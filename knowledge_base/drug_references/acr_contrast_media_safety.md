# American College of Radiology (ACR) Manual on Contrast Media

## 1. Intravenous Iodinated Contrast (CT Imaging)

### Renal Safety Guidelines (Contrast-Induced Nephropathy / CA-AKI)
- **Routine Renal Screening Threshold**:
  - Baseline serum creatinine / eGFR within 30-90 days is required for patients with:
    - History of known renal disease (eGFR < 60, single kidney, renal transplant)
    - Diabetes mellitus treated with medications
    - Severe chronic hypertension
    - Age > 60 years (depending on institutional policy)
- **eGFR Cutoffs**:
  - **eGFR ≥ 30 mL/min/1.73m²**: The risk of clinically significant CA-AKI is near zero. IV iodinated contrast is considered safe without mandatory prophylaxis.
  - **eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m² or Acute AKI**: Elevated risk of CA-AKI. Requires clinical risk-versus-benefit discussion. If contrast is essential (e.g., suspected acute aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism, active hemorrhage), **contrast should NOT be withheld for life-threatening emergencies**.
  - **Patients on Chronic Hemodialysis**: Can receive iodinated contrast. Contrast does not cause further damage to non-functioning native kidneys. Routine immediate post-CT dialysis is NOT required unless volume overload is an immediate danger.

### Metformin Management Protocol
- **Category 1: Patients with eGFR ≥ 45 mL/min/1.73m²**:
  - No need to discontinue metformin prior to or following iodinated contrast administration.
- **Category 2: Patients with eGFR 30–44 mL/min/1.73m² or undergoing intra-arterial catheterization**:
  - Discontinue metformin at the time of the procedure.
  - Re-evaluate eGFR 48 hours post-contrast; restart only if renal function is stable.
- **Category 3: Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) or eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m²**:
  - Metformin is already contraindicated. Discontinue immediately.

## 2. Gadolinium-Based Contrast Agents (GBCA - MRI Imaging)

### Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis (NSF) Risk Stratification
GBCAs are divided into 3 classes based on molecular stability (macrocyclic vs. linear chelate):
- **Group II Agents (Lowest Risk / Standard of Care)**:
  - Gadobutrol (Gadavist), Gadoterate meglumine (Dotarem), Gadoteridol (ProHance).
  - Risk of NSF is estimated at < 1 in 1 million even in severe renal failure.
  - Screening eGFR is **NOT mandatory** for Group II agents per ACR guidelines.
- **Group I Agents (High Risk - Linear Chelates)**:
  - Gadopentetate dimeglumine (Magnevist), Gadodiamide (Omniscan).
  - **Contraindicated** in severe CKD (eGFR < 30) or dialysis. Largely phased out of clinical practice.

## 3. Acute Contrast Reactions & Emergency Management

### A. Allergic-Like Reactions
- **Mild**: Scattered urticaria (hives), pruritus, nasal congestion, sneezing.
  - *Management*: Observation. Diphenhydramine (Benadryl) 25–50 mg PO/IV if symptomatic.
- **Moderate**: Diffuse erythema, facial/laryngeal edema with hoarseness, bronchospasm with mild wheezing.
  - *Management*: Oxygen 6–10 L/min via mask. Albuterol inhaler (2 puffs) for wheezing. Diphenhydramine 50 mg IV.
- **Severe**: Laryngeal stridor, severe bronchospasm, profound hypotension, anaphylactic shock, cardiac arrest.
  - *Immediate First-Line Drug*: **Epinephrine Intramuscular (IM)**
    - Concentration: 1:1,000 (1 mg/mL)
    - Dose: **0.3 mg IM** into anterolateral mid-thigh. Repeat every 5–15 minutes as needed.
    - IV Fluid bolus: 1000 mL 0.9% Normal Saline wide open.
    - Call Hospital Code / Emergency Response Team immediately.

### B. Contrast Extravasation
- Incidence: 0.1% to 0.9% of power-injected CT scans.
- Symptoms: Local pain, swelling, erythema, tight skin sensation.
- Management:
  - Stop injection immediately; aspirate residual contrast through catheter.
  - Elevate affected extremity above the level of the heart to promote lymphatic drainage.
  - Cold compresses initially to reduce localized inflammation.
  - Surgical consult required if: Compartment syndrome suspected (neurovascular deficit, paresthesias, pain on passive extension, loss of radial pulse).
