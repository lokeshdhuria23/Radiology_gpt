# Over-the-Counter (OTC) Analgesic, Antipyretic & Allergy Safety Reference

## 1. Non-Opioid Analgesics & Antipyretics

### Acetaminophen (Paracetamol, APAP)
- **Primary Indications**: Mild-to-moderate pain, fever reduction, musculoskeletal aches, tension headache. Preferred first-line antipyretic in elderly, renal impairment, gastrointestinal ulcer history, and pregnancy.
- **Mechanism of Action**: Central inhibition of prostaglandin synthesis through COX-3 / peroxidase pathway modulation. Does not inhibit peripheral cyclooxygenase, hence lacks significant peripheral anti-inflammatory action and does not impair platelet aggregation or gastric cytoprotection.
- **Adult Dosing**:
  - Regular Strength: 325 – 650 mg orally every 4 to 6 hours as needed.
  - Extra Strength: 500 – 1,000 mg orally every 6 hours as needed.
  - Maximum 24-Hour Dose: 3,000 mg in healthy ambulatory adults (2,000 mg in chronic liver disease, chronic alcohol consumption, or malnutrition).
- **Toxicity Warning**: Hepatic glutathione depletion leads to accumulation of the toxic electrophilic metabolite N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine (NAPQI), causing centrilobular hepatic necrosis. Check all combination cough/cold medications to avoid accidental overdose.

### Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs: Ibuprofen, Naproxen)
- **Primary Indications**: Inflammatory pain, joint arthralgia, dysmenorrhea, tension/sinus headache, fever with prominent inflammatory component.
- **Mechanism of Action**: Reversible non-selective inhibition of Cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1) and Cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), blocking conversion of arachidonic acid to pro-inflammatory prostaglandins and thromboxanes.
- **Adult Dosing**:
  - *Ibuprofen*: 200 – 400 mg orally every 4 to 6 hours with food. Maximum OTC dose: 1,200 mg/day (prescription maximum: 2,400 mg/day under clinical monitoring).
  - *Naproxen Sodium*: 220 mg orally every 8 to 12 hours with food. Maximum OTC dose: 660 mg/day.
- **Contraindications & Precautions**:
  - *Gastrointestinal*: Active or prior peptic ulcer disease, upper GI bleeding, gastritis.
  - *Renal*: Chronic Kidney Disease (eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73m²), congestive heart failure, severe dehydration. NSAIDs cause afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction, precipitating acute kidney injury (prerenal azotemia).
  - *Hematologic*: Thrombocytopenia (platelets < 100 × 10³/µL), concurrent oral anticoagulant therapy (warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban), active bleeding diathesis.
  - *Cardiovascular*: Increased risk of thrombotic cardiovascular events, fluid retention, worsening of hypertension.

## 2. Drug Hypersensitivity & Cross-Reactivity Protocols

### Beta-Lactam / Penicillin Hypersensitivity
- **Cross-Reactivity Profile**:
  - Aminopenicillins (Amoxicillin, Ampicillin) share similar side chains. True IgE-mediated anaphylaxis cross-reacts with first-generation cephalosporins (cephalexin, cefazolin) in ~2% of cases, and third-generation cephalosporins (ceftriaxone, cefdinir) in < 1%.
  - Carbapenems cross-react in < 1%.
- **Actionable Alert Rule**: Any patient with documented penicillin allergy must NOT receive Amoxicillin, Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin), Ampicillin, or Piperacillin-Tazobactam.

### Sulfonamide Hypersensitivity
- **Antibiotic Sulfonamides**: Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole (Bactrim/Septra), Sulfadiazine.
- **Non-Antibiotic Sulfonamides**: Furosemide, Hydrochlorothiazide, Celecoxib, Sumatriptan.
- **Clinical Fact**: Cross-reactivity between antibiotic sulfonamides (arylamine at N4 position) and non-antibiotic sulfonamides is extremely low, but caution is warranted in patients with documented Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) or Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN).

### Aspirin / NSAID Hypersensitivity (AERD)
- Patients with asthma and chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps who experience acute bronchospasm upon taking Aspirin or NSAIDs have Aspirin-Exacerbated Respiratory Disease (Samter's Triad).
- In these patients, ALL non-selective COX-1 inhibitors (ibuprofen, naproxen, ketorolac, diclofenac) are strictly contraindicated. Acetaminophen in low doses (<= 650 mg) is generally tolerated.
